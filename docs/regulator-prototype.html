  How this bench works
  
    Each arm can move in up to three motions (X, Y, Z), limited by the blueprint's tier. Pick an arm and pull a motion lever down: that motion is now driven. The crank sets the frequency of every driven motion at once, and the trim controls apply to them too. Let the crank go and it winds down, catching on whole-number ratios of the receiver's own frequency; simple ones grab hard, high ones barely hold.
    
  
    A lever pulled down starts at rest: spin the crank up to set it moving. Press Latch (or click a driven lever) to hold. The lever springs up but stays lit, and the motion keeps playing without listening to the crank or trim. Click a held lever to drive it again; if it's the only lever down the crank picks up its speed, otherwise it joins the others at the crank's current speed. To stop a motion, scroll the crank down below the first resonance. It ramps down and catches at rest, and latching there switches the motion off.
    
  
    The phase dial turns driven motions a quarter cycle, which is the difference between a circle and a line. Reach sets how far they swing and how loud their layer is; it shapes the figure but no recipe demands it. Any phase combination that draws the same figure counts, but a mirrored figure doesn't.
    
  
    When the figure matches the pinned blueprint it stands still, the reward layer ignites, and the voice lever unlocks. Near-misses of any recipe flicker faintly, including ones no blueprint shows.
